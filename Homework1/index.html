    Statistical Exploration & Cryptographic Discussion

    1. Distribution & Periodicity

    Observing the outputs for k = 1...8 with generator G = [1, 5], we notice strict constraints. The sequence is finite and cyclical[cite: 5]. The order of G is 9, meaning 9G = O (infinity)[cite: 5]. Coordinates are not uniform over [0, 16][cite: 5]. Because of the y² symmetry, every x is shared by two opposite y-coordinates[cite: 5], completely skewing the frequency distribution[cite: 5].

    2. Avalanche Effect & Entropy

    Hashes (like SHA-256) demand a ~50% Avalanche Effect[cite: 5]. If we shift the key from k = 6 (00110) to k = 7 (00111):

    • 6G = [5, 8] (00101 | 01000)
    • 7G = [10, 15] (01010 | 01111)

    7 out of 10 bits flipped (70%). While this seems hash-like, output entropy is extremely low (max ~4 bits)[cite: 5]. The high bit-flip is an artifact of the small modulo, not true cryptographic randomness.

    3. Algebraic Structure vs Hashes

    Elliptic curves are not hashes[cite: 5]. They preserve a strict algebraic group structure where (a+b)G = aG + bG[cite: 5]. This homomorphism allows digital signatures (ECDSA) to work[cite: 5]. Security relies entirely on the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (ECDLP)[cite: 5]. On secp256k1, the vast group order ($\approx 2^{256}$) makes generic attacks infeasible[cite: 5].
