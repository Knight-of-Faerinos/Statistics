Law of Large Numbers (LLN) & Central Limit Theorem (CLT) Simulation

Simulation Settings

1. Total Score (Sₙ)

📝 Interpretation: Divergence and Absolute Variance

  • Behavior: As the number of systems (n) increases, the trajectories continuously spread out. Mathematically, the variance of Sₙ grows linearly with n (Var(Sₙ) = n · Var(Xᵢ)).
  • Context: This represents the aggregate penetration score. Even though all attackers have the exact same skill level, sheer probability causes their successes to diverge wildly. A larger m reveals the extreme best-case and worst-case scenarios.

2. Average Score (Sₙ / n)

📝 Interpretation: Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

  • Convergence in Probability: The Law of Large Numbers states that the sample average converges in probability to the expected value (μ). The dashed red line represents this theoretical expectation.
  • Context: Early on, an attacker might seem highly successful or incompetent due to variance. As the system size (n) grows, "luck" is factored out, and the aggregate penetration score stabilizes to the attacker's true underlying probability.

3. Normalized Total Score (Sₙ / √n)

📝 Interpretation: Variance Stabilization

  • Variance Behavior: By dividing by √n, we perfectly balance the growing variance of the total score. The variance remains constant regardless of how large n gets, keeping trajectories bounded within a stable horizontal "tunnel".

4. Final Distribution (Proof of CLT)

Histogram of the final Normalized Scores (Sₙ / √n) at step n.

📝 Interpretation: Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

  • Convergence in Distribution: According to the CLT, the sum of independent identically distributed random variables, properly normalized, converges in distribution to a Gaussian (Normal) distribution.
  • Context: Looking at the endpoints of all attackers at the final step n, their scores form a bell curve. This implies that while individual penetration events are discrete and random, the macro-level behavior of many attackers is continuously distributed.

Final Conclusions: The Predictability of Risk

Through the simulation of n systems and m attackers, we draw three major conclusions:

  • Micro-Level Chaos vs. Macro-Level Order: At a small scale, penetration success is highly unpredictable. As targeted systems increase, the Law of Large Numbers anchors the average success rate to the true mathematical capability.
  • The Inevitability of the Bell Curve: The Central Limit Theorem reveals that the aggregate normalized scores always form a Gaussian distribution, allowing standard normal probabilities for worst-case calculations.
  • Systemic Risk is Quantifiable: The systemic risk of an infrastructure facing continuous threats is mathematically deterministic. Defenses can be optimized based on stable, long-term statistical limits rather than reacting to short-term anomalies.
